## Core safety principles

- Safety is critical in primary industries and rural work settings.
- Common hazards include vehicles, machinery, chemicals, and livestock.
- Farm safety is managed through training, risk awareness, Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), and safety rules.
- A safety-first culture is essential for the long-term wellbeing of the farm, the workers, and the wider rural community.

## Personal protective equipment (PPE)

- PPE stands for Personal Protective Equipment.
- Appropriate PPE must be used for the job being done.
- Examples include gloves, eye protection, hearing protection, closed footwear, and other task-specific gear.
- For chemical handling, PPE should be used when handling products, and a spill kit should be available nearby.

## Safe task and movement practices

- Lift heavy loads by bending the knees and using the leg muscles, not the back.
- Wear ear protection when using loud machinery such as chainsaws or tractors for long periods.
- Keep a planned escape route when working with large or unpredictable animals.
- Move quietly and slowly around animals and around the farm so that people and animals are not startled.
- Keep pathways clear to prevent trips, slips, and other site hazards.
- Clean and return tools to their proper storage so they do not become hazards for the next users.

## Livestock and animal care safety

- Approach animals calmly and from a safe position.
- Keep a clear escape path during animal checks and handling.
- Follow animal welfare routines such as checking water, replacing bedding, and maintaining clean feeding areas.
- Do not stand in a bee flight path; give bees space and watch from a safe distance.

## Chemical and storage safety

The source material includes clear rules for agricultural chemicals:

- Store chemicals in a locked cabinet or shed.
- Keep them in their original containers with clear labels.
- Use appropriate PPE when handling chemicals.
- Have a spill kit nearby.
- Keep chemicals away from animal feed and water sources.

## Daily farm task safety checks

The farm task checklist supports a practical safety routine:

- Wear heavy-duty gloves when handling gorse or sharp tools.
- Check watering troughs and refill with clean water.
- Replace bedding and maintain clean, safe animal shelters.
- Keep wood piles neat and stacked to reduce trip hazards and fire risks.
- Check tree guards and protect newly planted trees.
- Clear weeds and materials that could create hazards in work zones.
- Ensure all tools are cleaned, stored correctly, and returned to their hooks or racks.

## Common farm hazards to monitor

- Vehicles and quad bikes losing control
- Machinery noise and injury risk
- Chemical storage and accidental exposure
- Livestock handling risk
- Trips, slips, and cluttered work areas
- Sharp tools and heavy lifting
- Weather, mud, and uneven ground

## Safety expectation for the school farm

The wider repo documentation and safety resources point to the same expectation: students must work responsibly, follow induction and contract rules, complete hazard checks, and protect themselves, other people, and the farm environment. Safety is not a single rule; it is an ongoing routine of observation, preparation, and careful action.
