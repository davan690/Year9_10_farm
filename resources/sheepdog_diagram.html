Working Dog Guide

Essential Knowledge for New Farm Students

5 Safety Rules

  • 1

    Never approach or pet a working dog. When they are in the field, they are "on the clock." Petting them distracts from their crucial job and breaks their focus.

  • 2

    Do not feed the dogs. The handler strictly manages their diet and reward system. Unauthorized treats can disrupt their training and health.

  • 3

    Move calmly and predictably. Sudden movements, loud noises, or running can startle the livestock, making the dog's job exponentially harder and creating a dangerous environment.

  • 4

    Stay behind the handler. Unless instructed otherwise, always position yourself behind the handler. If you stand in the dog's path or between the dog and the stock, you will ruin the "draw."

  • 5

    Never give commands. Only the handler speaks to the dog. Multiple voices confuse the animal and undermine the handler's authority.

5 Useful Commands

"Come Bye"

The dog moves clockwise around the livestock. Think "C" for Clockwise and Come bye.

"Away to Me"

The dog moves counter-clockwise around the livestock.

"Walk Up"

The dog moves straight toward the livestock to apply pressure and move them forward.

"Stand" (or "Stop" / "Lie Down")

The dog immediately stops and holds its current position to release pressure on the stock.

"That'll Do"

The ultimate recall command. It means "your job is finished, leave the stock, and return directly to me."

The Balance Point

Understanding Handler-Dog-Stock Positioning

How it works: The dog instinctively wants to bring the stock to the handler. To move stock in a straight line toward the handler, the dog must find the "Balance Point" (exactly 180 degrees opposite the handler).

If the stock veers left, the dog moves left to apply pressure and correct their path. Your job as a student: Stay near the handler so you don't accidentally become a second "draw" and confuse the dog's geometry!
