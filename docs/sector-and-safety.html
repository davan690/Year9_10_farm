New Zealand Primary Industries

Key Vocabulary

Before diving into the activities, review the key terms below. You will need these to answer the questions in the following sections.

Biodiversity

The variety of plant and animal life in the world or in a particular habitat, a high level of which is usually considered important and desirable.

Horticulture

The branch of agriculture that deals with the art, science, technology, and business of growing plants (fruits, vegetables, flowers).

Pastoral Farming

Farming aimed at producing livestock (animals), such as dairy cattle, beef cattle, and sheep, rather than growing crops.

Primary Production

The sector of the economy involved in the extraction and production of raw materials, such as farming, logging, hunting, fishing, and mining.

Ruminant

A hoofed mammal (like a cow or sheep) that chews the cud and has a specialized stomach system to digest grass.

The Seven Key Sectors

Did you know? Primary production contributes billions of dollars to the New Zealand economy every year.

New Zealand’s primary industries are grouped into seven main sectors. Pastoral farming (Dairy, Beef, Sheep) is the largest, utilizing our grasslands. Horticulture covers fruit and vegetables, while Forestry manages timber. We also have Aquaculture / Seafood for fish, the Equine sector for horses, and Sports Turf management.

Focusing on Sustainability

As we learn about New Zealand’s primary industries, it’s vital to think about sustainability. This means managing our land, water, and resources in a way that is good for the environment, for people, and for the economy, both now and in the future. Let’s check your understanding of this important idea.

  1. The main goal of sustainable farming is to…?
    1. Produce the most food possible in one year.
    2. Use the newest and most expensive technology.
    3. Make sure we can farm successfully for many years without harming the environment.
    4. Only farm plants and not animals.
  2. Why is protecting biodiversity important for a farm?
    1. It makes the farm look nicer.
    2. It helps create a healthy ecosystem, which can improve soil and control pests naturally.
    3. It is required by law in every single case.
    4. It means the farmer can charge more for their products.
  3. Which of these is an example of a sustainable practice on a farm?
    1. Allowing cow manure and fertiliser to wash directly into rivers.
    2. Planting native trees along stream banks to filter water.
    3. Removing all trees to make bigger paddocks.
    4. Using as much water as possible for irrigation.
  4. How does using resources like water carefully help a farm in the long run?
    1. It doesn’t help; using more is always better.
    2. It ensures there will be enough water available for the farm in future dry years.
    3. It makes the grass grow slower.
    4. It is only important for farms near the ocean.

Sustainability Vocabulary

Thinking about safety and long-term success leads us to sustainability. This means using our resources wisely to ensure the primary industries can thrive for generations to come, protecting our environment. Match the sustainability terms from the word bank to their correct definitions below.

Word bank: Sustainability, Riparian Planting, Erosion, Ecosystem, Greenhouse Gas Emissions, Carbon Footprint

  1. The practice of meeting our own needs without preventing future generations from meeting theirs. It balances environmental, social, and economic factors.
  2. Planting trees and native plants along the banks of rivers, streams, and lakes to improve water quality and create habitats for wildlife.
  3. The gradual wearing away of land surface (soil) by wind, water, or other natural agents, which can be accelerated by farming practices.
  4. A biological community of interacting organisms (like plants and animals) and their physical environment.
  5. Gases like methane (from livestock) and carbon dioxide that are released into the atmosphere and contribute to climate change.
  6. The total amount of greenhouse gases generated by our actions, often related to an activity or an organisation.

Question 2: Resource Management and Future-Proofing

  • Connect the idea of a safe working environment to the broader goal of a sustainable and thriving rural community.

Agricultural Science Terms

Match the agricultural science terms from the word bank to their correct definitions below. These terms describe important processes and practices in farming.

Word bank: Photosynthesis, Germination, Fertiliser, Grazing System, Silage, Pest

  1. The process plants use to turn sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into food (energy).
  2. The process of a seed sprouting and beginning to grow into a plant.
  3. A substance (natural or chemical) added to soil to increase its fertility and help plants grow.
  4. A plan for how livestock are moved around paddocks to manage pasture growth.
  5. Grass or other green fodder compacted and stored in airtight conditions to be used as animal feed.
  6. An organism (like an insect or weed) that is harmful to crops, pasture, or livestock.

Question: Managing the Biological Cycle

“The success of any New Zealand farm, whether it is an orchard or a dairy farm, depends on the farmer’s ability to manage the natural biological processes of plants.”

  • Maximising use: Evaluate how a grazing system or the production of silage allows a farmer to effectively capture and store the energy produced by plants to feed animals later in the year.

Unit Summary: Fill in the Gaps

Working in New Zealand’s primary industries is more than just a job; it involves managing land, animals, and resources responsibly. For example, in pastoral farming, understanding how ruminants like cows digest pasture is crucial for feed management. In horticulture, knowing the right time to harvest crops ensures a good yield.

No matter the sector, safety is paramount. This means knowing the risks, from operating heavy machinery to handling agricultural chemicals. It’s about looking after yourself, your workmates, and the environment, ensuring the sector remains strong for future generations.
