Unit 6: Practical Skills Refraction - Refractometer Unit (Year 9-10)

Author

Urban Farm 2026

Published

Invalid Date

Unit overview

This unit uses practical farm produce testing to connect physics, biology, and measurement. Students use a handheld refractometer to measure sugar concentration (Brix) in fruit and vegetables collected from around the school farm.

Recommended duration: 4 lessons (40-60 min each)

Source resource and core concept

This unit is built from the worksheet in resources/Practical Skills Refraction 2026.pdf.

Core science idea from the worksheet

  • Light bends (refracts) when it moves between substances such as air, water, glass, or plastic.
  • Different amounts of dissolved substances bend light by different amounts.
  • Sugar is one dissolved substance we can test in food juices.
  • A refractometer measures sugar content in Degree Brix (% sucrose equivalent).
  • Higher Brix generally indicates greater sweetness and, in many crops, greater ripeness.

Why this matters on a farm

  • Farmers can estimate ripeness and harvest timing.
  • Brix helps compare crop quality across paddocks, varieties, and seasons.
  • Results support decisions about eating quality, processing, preserving, and market timing.

Learning goals

By the end of this unit, students can:

  • explain refraction in simple scientific language
  • calibrate and safely use a refractometer
  • collect and prepare juice samples from produce
  • take repeated measurements and calculate averages
  • compare measured values to a Brix reference chart
  • evaluate crop quality (poor, average, good, excellent)
  • communicate findings with evidence and farm recommendations

Detailed lesson plan

Lesson 1: Refraction and refractometer setup

Time: 50 min
Learning intention: Understand how light refraction allows sugar measurement.
Success criteria: Students explain Brix and correctly calibrate the instrument.

Lesson flow

  1. Hook and context (10 min)
  • Show refractometer and explain farm use.
  • Prompt: Why might farmers care about sugar levels?
  1. Teach concept (10 min)
  • Light bends between materials.
  • More dissolved solids change refraction and shift reading.
  • Degree Brix = approximate percent sucrose equivalent.
  1. Safety and handling (10 min)
  • Carry device with two hands.
  • Never scratch prism plate.
  • Clean with soft tissue and distilled water only.
  • No sharing droppers between unknown liquids without rinsing.
  1. Calibration practical (15 min)
  • Place distilled water on prism.
  • Close flap.
  • Read scale.
  • If needed, adjust top screw to zero (teacher supervised).
  1. Exit check (5 min)
  • Students write one sentence: How does a refractometer use light?

Resources

  • refractometers
  • distilled water
  • droppers/pipettes
  • lens tissue/soft wipes
  • gloves and eye protection as required

Lesson 2: Sample collection and Brix testing

Time: 50-60 min
Learning intention: Follow a valid sampling procedure and collect reliable data.
Success criteria: Students gather 3 repeat measurements for each selected item.

Procedure (from source worksheet)

  1. Select fruit/vegetables from around the farm.
  2. Prepare juice using a juicer or fork-mash and squeeze method.
  3. Place a small drop on prism.
  4. Close flap and take reading.
  5. Wipe prism with distilled water between every sample.
  6. Repeat readings for each item (at least three readings).
  7. Record and compute average Brix.

Group roles

  • sampler
  • juicer/prep lead
  • instrument operator
  • recorder/calculator
  • hygiene and cleanup checker

Lesson 3: Analysis and benchmarking

Time: 50 min
Learning intention: Compare measured values against typical Brix ranges.
Success criteria: Students classify at least two produce items and justify their judgement.

Required analysis questions (from source worksheet)

  1. Which food had the highest Brix measurement?
  2. Compare your results for at least two foods with typical measurements.
  3. Classify each as poor, average, good, or excellent.

Useful analysis extensions for Year 9-10

  • Range: max minus min for each item.
  • Reliability: discuss why repeats may vary.
  • Graphing: bar chart of average Brix by food type.
  • Evidence claim: Which crop area is producing sweetest produce and why?

Lesson 4: Farm decision making and communication

Time: 50 min
Learning intention: Use evidence to make practical farm recommendations.
Success criteria: Group presents clear recommendations linked to data.

Suggested presentation outputs

  • One-page farm quality report
  • Poster with table and graph
  • Short oral briefing to class/farm team

Required recommendation prompts

  • Which produce should be harvested now?
  • Which crops may need more time, water, or nutrient support?
  • What would you re-test next week to track change?

Assessment plan

Formative checks

  • Safe tool use and calibration accuracy
  • Correct cleaning between samples
  • Complete data table with repeated readings

Summative task

Group investigation report including:

  • method used
  • raw data and averages
  • comparison against reference ranges
  • quality classification (poor to excellent)
  • evidence-based farm recommendations

Health and safety controls

Risk Control
Glass/plastic instrument damage Handle over bench/tray, teacher demonstration first
Contamination between samples Distilled water rinse and wipe every sample
Food hygiene Gloves where needed, clean tools and surfaces
Allergies/sensitivities Pre-check allergies, avoid unsafe tasting
Slips/spills Immediate cleanup and clear walkways
Outdoor hazards during sampling Closed shoes, sun protection, supervised movement

Practical checklist

Reflection prompts

  • Which sample had the highest Brix and why do you think this happened?
  • Did your repeated readings stay close together? Explain.
  • What farm management action would you suggest based on your data?

Teacher notes and extension ideas

  • Re-test the same crop weekly and graph trend over time.
  • Compare sunny vs shaded growing areas.
  • Compare irrigation regimes where appropriate.
  • Integrate with economics: does higher Brix influence sale value?
  • Integrate with digital tech: students build a shared class dataset.
